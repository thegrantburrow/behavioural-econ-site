# My writing style

Reconstructed 2026-09-04 for this cloud environment from traits already
codified in `article-to-linkedin` (six feedback rounds), `authentic-voice`'s
LinkedIn-vs-site register note, and the two published posts that skill
produced (`linkedin-posts/the-tactile-premium/post.txt`,
`linkedin-posts/airds-high-money-talk/post.txt`). The original global profile
lived at `~/.claude/skills/my-writing-style/` on the Claude Code machine and
was not present in this cloud checkout; this file is the working profile going
forward. Update it whenever he rewrites a draft (see "Updating this profile").

## Who this is for

Drafts Grant will publish or send as himself. LinkedIn posts are the primary
surface. Do not apply this wholesale to Field Notes site prose (Principles,
Science Behind, Experiments, Special Reports): that register is restrained,
third-person, Bellroy/Vox-influenced. See `authentic-voice` for what transfers
and what doesn't.

## Core voice

- Warm, first person, spoken out loud rather than polished for a brand page.
- Exclamatory where a real feeling lands: "huge," "amazing," "fantastic,"
  "wonderful," "great." Not every sentence; when the moment earns it.
- Specific over vague. Real names, real numbers, real dates, real objects
  ("NRMA's latest Open Road magazine," "a pair of sneakers," "Year 8 and 9").
- Spaced hyphen for a mid-sentence aside (` - `), never an em dash.
- Ampersand is fine in short phrases ("print & posted," "acquisition &
  retention") when it matches how he'd type it; don't force it.
- "Eg" as a lead-in is his, leave it when he writes it.
- Doesn't over-polish: small mechanical slips that don't change meaning (a
  missing quotation mark, a dropped word) can stay. Real grammar errors (comma
  splices, dangling clauses) get fixed. Write grammatically sound sentences;
  expect him to tighten wording, not repair splices, on his own pass.

## Doc / LinkedIn / external

Structure for a LinkedIn post (the main surface this profile serves):

1. **No greeting.** No "Hi LinkedIn," no "Hope you're well." Straight into the
   moment.
2. **Short paragraphs with a blank line between each.** One idea per
   paragraph. A paragraph can end mid-thought on a trailing connective
   ("however...") and resolve in the next one when the next paragraph
   immediately delivers the payoff (cross-paragraph cliffhanger). Don't
   sprinkle that device for rhythm alone.
3. **Open on a concrete, dated personal moment**, then pivot to what the
   reader can do with the mechanism. Not "check out my latest piece." Not a
   report on the research itself as the reason for posting.
4. **Emoji-bulleted list for a small sample of findings or steps.** Numbered
   emoji (`1️⃣ 2️⃣ 3️⃣`) for ordered rules or top drivers. Topic emoji (`💰 📬 📚`)
   for a photo-caption aside or a finding. Keep the list short: for an
   article share, usually the top 2 drivers elaborated in plain language,
   with the citation/number as a parenthetical at the end of the bullet, not
   as the whole bullet. A third supporting finding can live in surrounding
   prose.
5. **Photo-caption asides**, when a post has a demo photo:
   - Right after the hook (demo caption): free sentence, no enclosing
     parentheses, trails into `...`
     (`💰 This is me marking a pair of sneakers down 50% off...`).
   - Later mid-post aside: spaced hyphen to introduce, plain smiley to close
     (` - a great side benefit :)`).
6. **Thanks to named collaborators** on live-event / workshop / talk recaps,
   early or near the close, with real names he has confirmed for *this*
   occurrence. Never invent a name or reuse last year's roster for a recurring
   program. If names aren't confirmed, leave a visible gap and say so in the
   review, don't guess.
7. **Closing link invitation** when the post points at site content, low-key
   and first-person: "I did a fuller write up with visuals here for those
   interested:" or, for a session recap, "If interested here are more details
   of the behavioural science behind it and some visuals". Plain URL, no
   tracking params. Not a formal citation label ("Full piece, with all the
   citations:").
8. **Close on one plain, specific thought**, not a tidy summary aphorism or a
   rule-of-three wrap-up. A real question to the reader, a genuine next step,
   or one observation. "Here's to next year." works when it's earned.
9. **Hashtag cluster at the end**, 5-7 tags, his real recurring set first:
   `#behaviouraleconomics` `#behaviouralscience` `#customers` `#experimentation`
   `#outcomes`, then topic-specific tags that are real industry terms
   (`#customerloyalty` `#directmail` `#money` `#school` `#mentoring` `#banking`
   `#product` `#financialwellbeing`), never a compound invented for the post.
   The cluster shifts by topic; for a recurring named program, prefer the
   hashtags he actually used on past posts about that same program.
10. **Length.** Standalone event-recap posts land around 150-350 words.
    Article-share posts that point at a full piece aim for roughly 120-220
    words so the link still has a reason to be clicked.

## Rigor habits that show up in his own edits

- Scope valuation claims accurately: "can be perceived as worth more" / "people
  valued and paid more," not flat "is worth more," when the backing study is
  WTP or perception.
- Qualify staged demos: "hypothetically spend," not bare "spend," when no real
  purchase happened.
- Parenthetical or bracketed notes he leaves inside returned copy that read as
  instructions to the drafter ("link to the article," "insert," "add") are
  directives, not copy. Resolve them. Everything else he returns is his prose
  and goes in verbatim, rough edges included.

## Surfaces this profile does not own

- Field Notes site articles (use `authentic-voice` + the relevant content-type
  skill). Third person except Field Sessions.
- Claude's own replies to him in chat.

## Applying this profile

Load this file before drafting any LinkedIn post or other text he will send as
himself. Match the "Doc / LinkedIn / external" section for structure. When a
documented habit doesn't fit the post type (e.g. collaborator thanks on an
article share with no event), drop it deliberately and name the gap when
presenting the draft. Invite correction. Prefer his own past posts about the
same recurring program over generic defaults when those exist.

## Updating this profile

When he rewrites a sentence through the copy-edit review, or posts a version
that differs from the draft, diff it. If the change is a pattern (a phrase he
always cuts, a rhythm he prefers, a fact he tightens, a device that evolved),
add it here the same day, not just into the one post. `article-to-linkedin`
folds pipeline-level defaults; this file holds voice-level ones. Keep both in
sync.
