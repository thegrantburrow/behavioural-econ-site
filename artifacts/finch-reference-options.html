A private reference library for your finch drawings

Somewhere to put every photo you draw from, and a way to find the right one again. Four ways to build it, then the decisions about what it actually does.

Every demo below is live, so click it. The colour extractor and the drawing view both take a real photo if you drop one in, and nothing you drop leaves your browser. Toggle each card, add notes, then hit Copy feedback at the bottom and paste it back to me.
First decision: where the library lives
Option A · my recommendation

A private page on claude.ai, with the photos stored inside it

You get one link. Open it on the laptop while you draw, open the same link on your phone when you are out and want to add a photo you just took. The page holds the photos itself, so there is no second service to sign into and nothing to keep in sync. Private by default, visible only when you are signed in as you, and it has to stay private for the saving to work at all.

  • Nothing to install, set up or run
  • Same link on phone and laptop
  • Drag photos straight in from either
  • I can build it today
  • There is a size ceiling, so the page shrinks each photo on the way in
  • Good for a few hundred references, not ten thousand
  • Your originals should still live somewhere else as the real backup

What adding a photo does: the page resizes it to reference resolution in your browser, reads the date off the file, pulls the colours out of it, saves it into the page, and drops you on the tagging row. No upload dialog, no folder to pick.

Option B

One file on your own machine, and the photos never leave it

A single HTML file you keep in your Documents folder and open by double clicking. It stores photos in the browser's own database on that machine, which means full resolution originals and far more room than any hosted option. Nothing ever touches a network. There is an export button that writes the whole library out as one backup file you can put in iCloud or Drive.

  • The most private option there is, since nothing leaves the device
  • Room for thousands of full size photos
  • Works with no internet at all
  • Fast, because nothing is being fetched
  • Tied to one machine and one browser
  • Photos on the laptop are not on your phone
  • Clearing browsing data wipes it, so the export is not optional

The trade in one line: Option A goes everywhere with you and holds less. Option B holds far more at full resolution and stays on one desk. If you draw at the same desk every time, this is the better tool.

Option C

A private repository behind a password, served as a real site

The photos live in a private GitHub repo and the site is served from Cloudflare with an access rule in front of it, the same setup already running your cricket site. Every photo is version controlled, so nothing is ever lost and you can see when each one went in. This is the archive option. It is the one that is still there in ten years.

  • A genuine backup with history, not just storage
  • Reachable from any device, still not public
  • No practical size ceiling
  • Nothing about it depends on claude.ai staying put
  • Adding a photo means a commit, unless I also build an upload endpoint
  • The most setup of the four
  • Adding from your phone is the awkward part

Worth saying plainly: this and Option A are not rivals. A sensible end state is Option A for the day to day and this underneath as the archive, which is a thing I can add later without rebuilding the front of it.

Option D

The photos stay in Google Drive and the page is only the filter

You already have a phone that puts photos into Drive. So one folder becomes the library, you add to it the way you already add to it, and the page reads that folder and gives you the filtering on top. The storage problem solves itself and so does uploading from your phone.

  • Adding a photo is just saving to a folder, which you already do
  • Drive's own storage, so no ceiling worth worrying about
  • Drive already backs it up and syncs it
  • Your tags have to live somewhere Drive will hold, which is fiddlier than it sounds
  • Slower, because every photo is fetched on demand
  • I would need to test the Drive connection before promising this one works

One honest caveat before you pick this. The page would reach Drive through your Claude connector, which is real and already attached to your account. I have not yet run a single call against it in this session, and I will not build a page on a guess about how it answers. If you want Option D, say so and I will test the connection first, then tell you whether it holds up before I build anything on it.

Second decision: what it actually does
Filtering

Everything you can slice by

You asked for date, subject and colour, and then for lots of other things. Here is my proposal for what the other things should be, chosen for what actually helps when you are looking for a photo to draw from. Picking two chips in the same row widens the search, picking chips in different rows narrows it. The demo is real, so try it.

12 of 12

Placeholder tiles, not photographs. The twelve tiles are drawn from each entry's colour palette so the filtering is honest to look at. Your real photos go in these slots. Also proposed and not shown here: a free text search over your own notes, a five star quality mark, and a drawn or not yet drawn flag so the library can show you only what you have never used.

Filtering

Colour, read out of the photo rather than typed in

Tagging colours by hand is the sort of job you do for eleven photos and then stop doing. So the page reads them instead: it samples the photo, sorts what it finds into named colours, and files it under those. A Gouldian lands under green, purple and yellow without you saying a word. Drop one of your own photos below and watch it happen. It runs in your browser and the photo goes nowhere.

You can still override it. The read is a starting point and it gets confused by a bright background, so every colour it assigns can be removed and you can add one it missed. It saves you the first ninety percent of a boring job rather than pretending to be right every time.

Adding photos

Adding a photo should take about four seconds

A library only works if adding to it is nearly free, because the moment it feels like admin you stop doing it and the library dies. So the page fills in everything it can read off the file, and leaves you three quick taps. You can also drop in thirty photos at once and tag them as a batch, since a morning's photographs are usually the same bird in the same light.

Using it

The view you actually draw from

Finding the photo is only half of it. Once it is open, the page should do the things you would otherwise do by hand: strip the colour out so you can read the values, lay a grid over it for proportions, flip it to catch the errors your eye has stopped seeing, and hold the palette next to it so you can mix from it. All four buttons below work. Drop in one of your own photos and they work on that instead.

Values only is the one worth arguing for. Half of what makes a drawing read as solid is tone rather than colour, and colour hides tone. One tap answers the question you would otherwise squint at. Flip is the old trick of holding a drawing up to a mirror, which is the fastest way to see that a head is too small.

Scope

How much of this should exist by tonight

Everything above is buildable, and building all of it at once means you wait and then review a large thing all in one go. My suggestion is the lean version first: upload, the automatic date and colour reads, the six filter rows, the grid, and the drawing view with values and flip. That is a tool you can start filling tonight, and filling it is what tells us which of the rest you actually want.

  • Lean first: you use it this week and the next round comes from real use
  • Everything at once: batch tagging, drawn or not flags, notes, search, export, all of it before you touch it
