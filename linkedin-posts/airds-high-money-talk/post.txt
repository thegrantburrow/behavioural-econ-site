Why do smart 14-year-olds still think "50% off" means they're saving money?

Got to find out first hand, running an "Everyday Banking" session for a room of Year 8 and 9 students at Airds High School.

Opened with a $5 chocolate bar. Marked it down live, in front of them, to $2.50. Asked the room how much they were saving.

Hands shot up: "$2.50."

Wrong. Nobody in that room had $5 in their hand a minute earlier. They weren't saving $2.50, they were about to spend it. That's anchoring. Getting the wrong answer out loud, then corrected on the spot, is what actually stuck.

From there, three habits simple enough to run without a spreadsheet or an app:

1️⃣ Split whatever comes in three ways before deciding what to do with any of it: spend, save for something specific, save for later.

2️⃣ Name each third before it reaches your hand. "Save for Phone" survives being spent. "Savings Account 2" doesn't.

3️⃣ Hide the ones you're not using. Less visible, less spent.

We simulated ten weeks with real coins into hand labelled envelopes. By the end, $30 had landed in the "save" envelopes that nobody had consciously decided to save.

None of this needs a classroom. The same three habits run through Pockets, Spaces, or Sub-accounts most banking apps already have, the only real ingredient is deciding the split before the money's in your hand, not after.
