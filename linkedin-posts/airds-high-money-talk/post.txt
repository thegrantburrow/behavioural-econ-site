It was wonderful to do a short interactive talk this week with students from Airds High School, part of NAB's Everyday Banking program with Early Careers, giving a room of Year 8 and 9 students a hands-on look at what working in a bank is really like.

(💰 This is me marking a $5 chocolate bar down to $2.50, live, in front of them, to make a point about how our brains judge a "deal" :)

I asked the room how much they were saving. Hands shot up: "$2.50." Wrong, nobody in that room had $5 in their hand a minute earlier, they were about to spend it, not save it. Getting that one out loud, then corrected on the spot, did more work than any definition I could have given them first.

Money and banking can be complex for anyone (not least students!), so from there I shared three simple rules for saving, the same ones I use at home (I tried not to labour the point, but these draw from real behavioural science):

1️⃣ A third, a third, a third (split whatever comes in three ways: spend now, save for something specific, save for later)
2️⃣ Name your money (a labelled "Save for Phone" envelope survives being spent, "Savings Account 2" doesn't)
3️⃣ Out of sight, out of mind (hide the ones you're not using, less visible means less spent)

We simulated ten weeks with real coins into hand-labelled envelopes, and by the end $30 had landed in the "save" envelopes that nobody had consciously decided to save.
