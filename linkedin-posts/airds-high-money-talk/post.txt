It was wonderful to do a short interactive talk recently with students from Airds High School, part of NAB's program with Early Careers, giving a room of Year 8 and 9 students a hands-on look at what working in a bank is really like.

💰 This is me marking a pair of sneakers down 50% off to make a point about how our brains judge a "deal" ...

I asked the room how much they were saving - the answer seems to be 50%?", however...

While it can feel like we're saving, they were actually about to hypothetically spend money they hadn't planned on.

That's the common principle of anchoring where the discount only feels real because of the number sitting right next to it.

We then had an interaction with my favourite teacher, Mr George…

Money and banking can be complex for anyone (not least students!), so from there I shared three simple rules for saving, the same ones I use at home with my wonderful children that draw from real behavioural science:

1️⃣ A third, a third, a third (split whatever comes in three ways: spend now, save for something specific, save for later)
2️⃣ Name your money (a labelled "Save for Phone" envelope survives being spent, "Savings Account 2" doesn't)
3️⃣ Out of sight, out of mind (hide the ones you're not using, less visible means less spent)

We simulated ten weeks with real coins into hand-labelled envelopes, and by the end $30 had landed in the "save" envelopes that nobody had consciously decided to save.

A number of the adults shared how helpful the session was for them too - a great side benefit :)

Big thanks to Jon Singam and all those involved at NAB with this wonderful program. It's great seeing how much the students get out of each one. Huge thanks to the team of colleagues and volunteers who helped make the day happen.

Here's to next year.
