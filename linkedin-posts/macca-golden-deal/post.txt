Last week I opened the McDonald's app and there it was - a $2.50 large fries deal, pickup only, tagged Today's Golden Deal and expiring at midnight.

Sitting right under the price: "Plus, a chance to WIN!" Free Macca's for *life*.

It got me thinking about how a tiny chance at something vivid can turn a plain discount into a completely different moment.

On paper it's still $2.50 fries. Attach a prize draw and the mind stops weighing the real, probably tiny odds correctly - same shape as a lottery ticket, or a bank savings promo that puts a chance at a jackpot rate next to the guaranteed one.

If a missing odds line shouldn't move a $2.50 decision that much - what might be going on?

There's a commercial version of this hiding in plain sight - acquisition, retention, and how a chance is framed before anyone "decides."

A few active ingredients that might be going on:

🎰 Probability Weighting - once "a chance to win" is attached, almost any small probability feels worth chasing
🏷️ Framing - "free food for life" vs "$30 a week for 40 years" (about $62,400, eight McGold Cards Australia-wide) is the same prize landing as two different offers
😊 Anticipatory joy - simply holding a lottery-style ticket lifts happiness before the draw, win or lose

There's real research behind those - Kahneman & Tversky on overweighting small probabilities, Rottenstreich & Hsee on affect-rich prizes, Burger and colleagues on the measured happiness lift from holding a ticket.
