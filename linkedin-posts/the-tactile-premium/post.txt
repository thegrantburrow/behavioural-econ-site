Two days ago I received NRMA's latest Open Road magazine in the post.

Same magazine they've been printing since 1927, still landing in letterboxes on top of the digital hub NRMA built for it back in 2025.

Got me thinking about the behavioural science behind why the printed copy still earns its place, and a couple of practical ways you could put the same thing to work yourself.

1️⃣ Touch creates a sense of ownership before you've bought anything: briefly holding something, or even just picturing holding it, makes you value it more and pay more for it (Peck & Shu, across four separate studies).

2️⃣ Identical content is worth more once it's physical: across five separate experiments, people valued and paid more for a physical version of the exact same content than the digital one (Atasoy & Morewedge).

The part that ties it together for me: a newer study, from two of the same researchers, found that same feeling of ownership predicts real customer loyalty, independent of how satisfied someone actually is. If you're working on acquisition or retention, that's worth testing with something as small as a printed welcome card, or letting people customise one physical part of what they get.

Digital's still going to be cheaper. Just worth asking whether cheaper is actually the outcome you're optimising for.
