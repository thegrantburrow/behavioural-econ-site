Two days ago I received NRMA's latest Open Road magazine in the post.

It's the same magazine they've been printing and sending to mailboxes since 1927 even with their modern era digital content hub.

It got me thinking about the behavioural science behind why the printed copy may still be important - and the practical real world applications for all organisations.

1️⃣ Touch creates a sense of ownership before you've bought anything.

Eg briefly holding something or even just picturing holding it makes you value it more and pay more for it (Peck & Shu, across four separate studies).

2️⃣ Identical content can be perceived as worth more once it's physical.

Across five separate experiments people valued and paid more for a physical version of the exact same content than the digital one (Atasoy & Morewedge).

A newer study, from two of the same researchers, found that same feeling of ownership predicts real customer loyalty, independent of how satisfied someone actually is.

If you're working on acquisition or retention, that's worth testing with something as small as a printed welcome card, or letting people customise one physical part of what they get.

Digital's still going to be cheaper. Just worth asking whether cheaper is actually the outcome you're optimising for.
