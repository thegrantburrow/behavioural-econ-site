A little while back I stood in front of a room of Year 8 and 9 students and handed round three actual envelopes: Spend for Now, Save for Phone, Save for Future.

Split every pay three ways. Name each third. Hide the one meant to last. Each of those three rules borrows its own separately tested finding from behavioural economics.

Sharing it because you can run the practical version tonight, in whatever banking app you already have open.

1️⃣ Timing beats willpower. Deciding the split the moment pay lands, before it feels spent, is the trick behind "Save More Tomorrow" - a real workplace program that nearly quadrupled average savings rates (3.5% to 13.6%) by timing it against a future pay rise instead of today's pay.

2️⃣ A name changes how money gets treated. The identical $10 loss is treated completely differently depending on which "account" it's framed as coming from, a reframe that swung real behaviour by 42 points (46% to 88%). "Save for Phone" beats "Savings 2" for a reason.

Hiding that third account, and setting a reminder that names the actual goal, works roughly twice as well as a generic "check your savings" nudge.

None of this needs a new app. It needs three renamed accounts and a standing transfer on payday.
